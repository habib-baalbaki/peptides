Research overview

GHK-Cu is the copper(II) complex of the tripeptide Gly-His-Lys, first identified in human plasma. Cell-culture and animal wound-model studies have examined it in relation to collagen and extracellular-matrix synthesis, and gene-expression analyses report that GHK is associated with changes in the expression of a large number of genes[1].

KPV (Lys-Pro-Val) is the C-terminal tripeptide of alpha-melanocyte-stimulating hormone (α-MSH). In mouse colitis models it was taken up by intestinal epithelial cells through the PepT1 transporter and reduced markers of intestinal inflammation[2].

BPC-157 is a 15-amino-acid synthetic peptide whose sequence is taken from a larger protein (body protection compound) isolated from gastric juice. Most of the published work is in rodent models of gastrointestinal, tendon, ligament, muscle and bone injury, where it has been studied alongside angiogenic growth-factor signalling[3][4]. A 2025 systematic review found 36 eligible studies, 35 of them preclinical and one clinical, and no clinical safety data[5].

TB-500 in this blend is the synthetic fragment Ac-LKKTETQ, residues 17–23 of thymosin β4 (Tβ4), the region of the protein that binds actin (889 Da by independent lab analysis). Full-length Tβ4, a 43-amino-acid G-actin-sequestering peptide, has been investigated in experimental models of wound repair, angiogenesis, and corneal and cardiac injury[6].

None of these compounds is authorized by Health Canada as a drug. The literature summarised here is preclinical unless stated otherwise, and it is provided as background for laboratory researchers, not as evidence of any effect in people.

KLOW FAQ

What is in KLOW?

KLOW is a four-peptide research blend: GHK-Cu (50 mg), KPV (10 mg), BPC-157 (10 mg) and TB-500 (10 mg), 80 mg in one lyophilized vial.

What is KPV?

KPV (Lys-Pro-Val) is the C-terminal tripeptide of alpha-melanocyte-stimulating hormone. It has been studied in mouse models of intestinal inflammation. Molecular weight 342.4 g/mol, CAS 67727-97-3.

Which form of TB-500 is in KLOW?

The short fragment Ac-LKKTETQ (thymosin β4 residues 17–23, 889 Da), as reported by Janoshik Analytical for this batch. Our standalone TB-500 vial is full-length thymosin β4.

What is the difference between KLOW and GLOW?

KLOW is GLOW plus 10 mg of KPV. Both contain GHK-Cu, BPC-157 and TB-500 in the same amounts.

How is each lot of KLOW tested?

Every lot is tested by an independent laboratory (Janoshik Analytical) for purity (HPLC) and identity (mass spectrometry). The lot number printed on the vial matches the certificate of analysis on our lab results page.

How should lyophilized KLOW be stored?

Keep sealed vials refrigerated at 2–8 °C, dry and away from light. For storage longer than a few months, many laboratories keep lyophilized peptides frozen at −20 °C. Let the vial reach room temperature before opening to limit moisture uptake.

Is KLOW approved for use in people?

No. KLOW is not authorized by Health Canada or the US FDA as a drug. It is sold strictly for in-vitro and laboratory research. We do not provide dosing, administration or usage guidance.
