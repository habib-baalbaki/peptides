Research overview

MOTS-c (mitochondrial open reading frame of the 12S rRNA-c) is a 16-amino-acid peptide encoded within mitochondrial DNA, in the gene for 12S ribosomal RNA. In the study that first described it, its main target appeared to be skeletal muscle, where it inhibited the folate cycle and de novo purine synthesis, leading to AMPK activation; in mice it was investigated in models of age-dependent and high-fat-diet-induced insulin resistance and of diet-induced obesity[1]. Later work reported that exercise raised endogenous MOTS-c in human skeletal muscle and plasma, and studied MOTS-c treatment in mouse models of age-related decline in physical capacity[2].

None of these compounds is authorized by Health Canada as a drug. The literature summarised here is preclinical unless stated otherwise, and it is provided as background for laboratory researchers, not as evidence of any effect in people.

MOTS-c FAQ

What is MOTS-c?

MOTS-c is a 16-amino-acid peptide (MRWQEMGYIFYPRKLR) encoded by mitochondrial DNA, first described in 2015. Its name stands for mitochondrial open reading frame of the 12S rRNA-c. It is studied mainly in mouse models of metabolism and ageing.

What is the molecular weight of MOTS-c?

2174.6 g/mol. Its molecular formula is C101H152N28O22S2 and its CAS number is 1627580-64-6 (PubChem CID 146675088).

Why is MOTS-c called a mitochondrial-derived peptide?

Almost all proteins are encoded by nuclear DNA. MOTS-c is encoded by a short open reading frame inside the mitochondrial genome, in the 12S rRNA gene. Humanin is another peptide of this class.

How is each lot of MOTS-c tested?

Every lot is tested by an independent laboratory (Janoshik Analytical) for purity (HPLC) and identity (mass spectrometry). The lot number printed on the vial matches the certificate of analysis on our lab results page.

How should lyophilized MOTS-c be stored?

Keep sealed vials refrigerated at 2–8 °C, dry and away from light. For storage longer than a few months, many laboratories keep lyophilized peptides frozen at −20 °C. Let the vial reach room temperature before opening to limit moisture uptake.

Is MOTS-c approved for use in people?

No. MOTS-c is not authorized by Health Canada or the US FDA as a drug. It is sold strictly for in-vitro and laboratory research. We do not provide dosing, administration or usage guidance.
