Research overview

TB-500 is the research-supply name for synthetic thymosin β4 (Tβ4), a 43-amino-acid peptide present in most cells that binds and sequesters G-actin. The name is also used elsewhere for a short Tβ4 fragment; ours is the full-length peptide, identified by independent lab analysis. Tβ4 has been investigated in experimental models of wound repair, angiogenesis, and corneal and cardiac injury[1].

None of these compounds is authorized by Health Canada as a drug. The literature summarised here is preclinical unless stated otherwise, and it is provided as background for laboratory researchers, not as evidence of any effect in people.

TB-500 FAQ

What is TB-500?

TB-500 is the research-supply name for synthetic thymosin β4, a 43-amino-acid actin-binding peptide (molecular weight about 4963 g/mol, CAS 77591-33-4). Thymosin β4 has been studied in experimental models of wound repair and angiogenesis.

Is TB-500 the same as thymosin β4?

It depends on the supplier. Some sell a short seven-amino-acid fragment of thymosin β4 (Ac-LKKTETQ, about 889 g/mol) under the name TB-500. Ours is full-length thymosin β4, as identified by independent lab analysis of the batch.

How is each lot of TB-500 tested?

Every lot is tested by an independent laboratory (Janoshik Analytical) for purity (HPLC) and identity (mass spectrometry). The lot number printed on the vial matches the certificate of analysis on our lab results page.

How should lyophilized TB-500 be stored?

Keep sealed vials refrigerated at 2–8 °C, dry and away from light. For storage longer than a few months, many laboratories keep lyophilized peptides frozen at −20 °C. Let the vial reach room temperature before opening to limit moisture uptake.

Is TB-500 approved for use in people?

No. TB-500 is not authorized by Health Canada or the US FDA as a drug. It is sold strictly for in-vitro and laboratory research. We do not provide dosing, administration or usage guidance.
